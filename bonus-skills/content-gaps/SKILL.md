# Finding what you should have written about

## When to Use
When I ask what we should write about, or for
content gaps.

## Procedure
1. Read ~/Business/Marketing/site-map.md. If it has a "not for
   us" list, note it.
2. Review what the named competitors publish. Use only the
   competitor pages and titles I have given you or that
   are listed in site-map.md (change this to wherever your own
   competitor list lives).
3. Find subjects a named competitor answers that we don't, and
   that our customers would plausibly search for. Every gap must
   have a competitor page behind it. Leave out anything on the
   "not for us" list.
4. Give me up to ten gaps, ranked by relevance to what we sell,
   each with one line on what the piece would need to say and the
   title of the competitor page that prompted it. If fewer than
   ten gaps have a competitor page behind them, give fewer and
   say so.
5. Put subjects you thought of yourself, with no competitor page
   behind them, in a separate short list headed "My own ideas,
   no competitor page". They do not count towards the ten.
6. Never name any customer, client or person of ours in the list.

## Pitfalls
- Do not report search volumes unless a real data source is
  connected. Estimated volumes invented by a model are
  worthless and will mislead planning.
- A subject we cover badly is not a competitor-backed gap. If
  you think one is worth writing again, put it under "My own
  ideas, no competitor page".
- Do not say the whole list came from the competitors' pages
  unless every item did.

## Verification
For each gap, name the competitor page that prompted it. Check
that no item in the main list lacks one, and that no customer or
client is named.

## Approval Required
None; the list comes to me. Never send, pay, post or delete
anything without my explicit "approved".
