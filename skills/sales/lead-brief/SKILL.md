# Every inbound lead, researched before you reply

## When to Use
When an enquiry arrives, or I ask for a lead brief or who a
prospect is.

## Procedure
1. Extract the company and contact from the enquiry.
2. Research the company: what they do, rough size, how long
   they've been going, anything recent worth mentioning.
3. Tell me which of our services fits, and a rough deal-size
   range based only on comparable past quotes, labelled as an
   estimate (or "no comparable work").
4. Give me one specific opening line that proves I looked them
   up.
5. Keep the whole brief to half a page. Do not reply to them.

## Pitfalls
- Common company names return the wrong business. Cross-check
  against the domain in the enquirer's email address before
  reporting anything.
- If the research turns up nothing credible, say so plainly. A
  confident brief about the wrong company is worse than none.
- Never include the opening line in a draft reply to the
  prospect — it goes to me, for me to use or discard.
- A deal-size range is an estimate from our own past quotes,
  never a valuation. Show the comparables it rests on, and say
  "no comparable work" rather than inventing a figure.

## Verification
Every factual claim in the brief carries its source URL.

## Stop and Ask Me Instead When
- The enquiry mentions an acquisition, a legal matter or a named
  private individual. Do not search for those names; tell me why
  you stopped.
- The enquirer is already a client or in my contacts. Skip the
  web research and summarise what we already know.

## Approval Required
None; the brief comes to me only. Never send, pay, post or
delete anything without my explicit "approved".
